## Script

NASA flew a spacecraft all the way to Mars, and lost it on arrival. The cause was a unit conversion.

Mars Climate Orbiter launched in December 1998. About nine months later, it was due to enter orbit, passing 226 kilometers above Mars.

Ground software supplied by Lockheed Martin reported thruster impulse in pound-force seconds. NASA's navigation software read those numbers as newton-seconds.

One pound-force second is 4.45 newton-seconds. So every thruster firing was underestimated, and the predicted path drifted away from the real one.

At least two navigators noticed the discrepancy. Their concerns were dismissed, because they had not filled out the right form.

On September 23rd, 1999, the orbiter came within about 57 kilometers of the surface. It needed at least 80 to survive.

The spacecraft was lost. The orbiter and its companion lander cost $327 million.
