# One changed steel rod killed 114 people

## Description

On July 17, 1981, two suspended walkways collapsed in the Hyatt Regency hotel in Kansas City, Missouri. A change from one continuous hanger rod to two offset rods doubled the force on a single connection.

## Script

One small change to a steel rod killed 114 people.

July 17th, 1981. About 1,600 people fill the atrium of the Hyatt Regency in Kansas City for a dance.

Above them hang two walkways, one directly over the other. The design: one continuous rod from the ceiling, through the fourth floor walkway, down to the second.

The steel fabricator proposed two shorter rods instead. But now the lower walkway hangs from the upper walkway's beam, not from the ceiling.

That doubled the force on one connection. The beam could carry only 30% of the load it now had to hold. Even the original design reached only 60% of what the building code required.

At about 7:05 that evening, the connection tore through. Both walkways fell onto the crowded floor.

114 people died and 216 were injured. The engineering firm lost its licenses in three states.
