# Why golf balls have dimples

**File:** `023-golf-ball-dimples.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 52.7 s

## Description

Dimples make the thin layer of air on a golf ball turbulent, so it stays attached longer and leaves a narrower low-pressure wake, which means less drag. They also increase the lift from backspin. Most modern balls have about 300–500 dimples.

## Script

Why are golf balls covered in dimples? Because a smooth ball flies worse. Really.

Golfers found out by accident. Old balls, nicked from use, flew more consistently than smooth new ones. So makers began cutting marks in on purpose.

Here is why. Air leaves a smooth ball early, and opens a wide, low pressure wake behind it. That wake drags the ball back.

Dimples stir up the thin layer of air on the surface. Now it hangs on for longer. The wake narrows, and the drag drops.

There is a second effect. Backspin bends the air downward, and that creates lift, a little like a wing.

Most modern balls have between 300 and 500 dimples.
