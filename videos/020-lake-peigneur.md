# The oil rig that drained a whole lake

**File:** `020-lake-peigneur.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 52.5 s

## Description

On November 20, 1980, a drilling rig working for Texaco pierced a salt mine under Lake Peigneur, Louisiana. The lake drained into the mine, swallowing the rig, 11 barges, a tugboat and 65 acres of land. All 55 miners escaped and nobody was killed.

## Script

How do you drain an entire lake by accident? You drill a hole in the bottom.

Louisiana, 1980. Lake Peigneur is just ten feet deep. And an oil rig is drilling there.

But under the lake, there is a salt mine. The 14 inch drill goes straight into it.

The lake pours into the mine. A whirlpool forms. It swallows the rig, eleven barges, a tugboat, and 65 acres of land.

The canal that drained the lake ran backwards, pulling in the sea. For a few days, it made a waterfall 164 feet tall.

All 55 miners got out. Nobody died. And investigators never settled which was wrong: the drilling, or the mine's maps.
