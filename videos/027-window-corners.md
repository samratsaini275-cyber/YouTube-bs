## Script

In 1954, a jet airliner broke apart in mid-air over the Mediterranean. All 35 people on board were killed.

It was the de Havilland Comet. The world's first jet airliner.

Investigators pieced it back together. A crack had grown from one tiny rivet hole, at the corner of a window-shaped opening in the roof.

Here is the problem with corners. Stress flows around an opening, like water around a rock. At a sharp corner, it piles up.

Every flight, the cabin is pumped up with air and then let down again. Each time, a crack can creep a little further.

That is why jet windows have big, rounded corners. The stress spreads out, instead of piling up. Fun fact: it was not the passenger windows that failed on the Comet. But the lesson stuck.
