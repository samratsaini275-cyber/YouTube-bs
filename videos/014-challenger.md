# A cold rubber ring destroyed Challenger

**File:** `014-challenger.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 79.2 s

## Description

Space Shuttle Challenger broke apart 73 seconds into its flight on January 28, 1986, killing all seven crew members. The cause was the failure of the primary and secondary O-ring seals in a joint of the right solid rocket booster, on a record-cold launch morning.

## Script

This rocket was sealed with rings of rubber. One cold morning in 1986, they failed, and seven astronauts died.

The Space Shuttle's solid rocket boosters were built in segments. At each joint, two rubber O-rings sealed in the burning gas.

Rubber stiffens in the cold. And a stiff O-ring is less able to seal the joint.

The coldest shuttle launch until then was at 62 degrees Fahrenheit. The night before this one, engineers at the booster maker recommended against launching.

The launch went ahead, at 36 degrees. Less than a second after ignition, puffs of dark smoke came from a joint on the right booster.

About 59 seconds into the flight, a flame appeared at that joint. It burned through to the external fuel tank.

73 seconds into the flight, Challenger broke apart, 46,000 feet above the Atlantic. All seven crew members were killed.

The cause was the failure of both O-ring seals in that one joint. Shuttle flights stopped for 32 months.
