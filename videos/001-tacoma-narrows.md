## Script

This bridge tore itself apart in a 40 mile an hour wind. And the textbook explanation for why is wrong.

The Tacoma Narrows Bridge opened in July 1940. It was the third longest suspension bridge in the world.

To keep it slender, the deck was stiffened with solid steel girders only 8 feet deep, instead of the 25 foot open trusses first proposed.

The deck bounced in the wind so much that construction workers nicknamed it Galloping Gertie.

On November 7th, the motion changed. The deck began to twist. That is aeroelastic flutter. The motion itself draws energy from the wind, so every twist grows.

It was not simple resonance, the textbook explanation. That morning, the main span broke up and fell into Puget Sound.

The only death was a cocker spaniel named Tubby. A replacement bridge opened ten years later, in 1950.
