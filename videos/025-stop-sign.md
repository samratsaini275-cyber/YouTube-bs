# Why stop signs have eight sides

**File:** `025-stop-sign.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 50.0 s

## Description

The stop sign's unique eight-sided shape lets drivers facing the back of it tell that oncoming drivers have a stop sign, and it could be identified at night before signs were reflective. The octagon was chosen in 1922; the sign was yellow until 1954.

## Script

A stop sign has eight sides. No other road sign looks like it. And that is the whole point.

See it from behind, and you can still tell what it is. So you know the other driver has to stop.

The shape worked at night, too. Early signs were not reflective. But an octagon could still be picked out in the dark.

The first stop sign went up in Detroit, in 1914. It was a white square, with black letters.

In 1922, a committee chose the octagon. But the sign was not red. It was yellow.

Why yellow? Red that would not fade did not exist yet. Red only became the rule in 1954.
