# A 767 ran out of fuel at 41,000 feet over a unit mix-up

**File:** `006-gimli-glider.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 53.1 s

## Description

On July 23, 1983, Air Canada Flight 143, a Boeing 767, ran out of fuel at 41,000 feet after a pounds/kilograms conversion error and glided to a landing at Gimli, Manitoba. All 69 people on board survived.

## Script

At 41,000 feet, this passenger jet ran out of fuel. The reason was one wrong number.

July 23rd, 1983. Air Canada Flight 143. The fuel gauges are not working, so the fuel is measured by hand.

To turn litres into kilograms, the crew multiplied by 1.77. But that figure was pounds per litre. The right one was about 0.8 kilograms per litre.

The aircraft needed 22,300 kilograms of fuel. It took off with 22,300 pounds. Less than half.

Halfway to Edmonton, both engines stopped. A small wind turbine dropped from the fuselage to power the hydraulics.

The captain, an experienced glider pilot, brought the jet down on a former air force runway at Gimli, where a car race was underway.

All 69 people on board survived. The aircraft was repaired, and flew until 2008.
