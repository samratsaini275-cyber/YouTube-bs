# Half-inch steel plates doomed this highway bridge

## Description

The I-35W Mississippi River bridge in Minneapolis collapsed at 6:05 p.m. on August 1, 2007, killing 13 people and injuring 145. The NTSB cited undersized gusset plates, a design error, with the weight on the bridge as a contributing factor.

## Script

This highway bridge carried a design error for 39 years. Then, in rush hour, it fell into the Mississippi River.

The I-35W bridge in Minneapolis opened in 1967, and carried 140,000 vehicles a day.

Its steel truss members were joined by flat plates, called gusset plates. A design error left some of them undersized, at just half an inch thick.

The bridge stayed in service for 39 years. Over that time, two inches of added concrete raised its static load by 20%.

On August 1st, 2007, 578,000 pounds of construction materials and equipment sat on the deck.

At 6:05 in the evening rush hour, a gusset plate ripped along a line of rivets. 111 vehicles were on the bridge.

13 people died, and 145 were injured. A replacement bridge opened in September 2008.
