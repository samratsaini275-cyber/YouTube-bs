# A 59-story tower was quietly repaired at night

## Description

In 1978 a structural flaw was found in the newly completed 59-story Citicorp Center in New York. Bolted joints had replaced welded ones, and the tower was vulnerable to quartering winds. It was reinforced at night; the story became public in 1995.

## Script

In 1978, a brand new 59 story skyscraper in New York could have been blown over. The public found out 17 years later.

Citicorp Center stands on four stilts. They sit under the middle of each side, not the corners, to leave room for a church.

Its wind braces were designed with welded joints. To save money, they were built with bolted joints instead.

That year, students studying the tower asked about quartering winds, which strike a corner diagonally. Engineer William LeMessurier rechecked the design.

His estimate: if a power failure stopped the tower's mass damper, a storm strong enough to topple it had a one in 16 chance of arriving in any year.

Starting that August, crews welded steel plates over the bolted joints, at night. Hurricane Ella threatened New York during the work, then changed course.

The repairs were finished that October. The story became public in a New Yorker article in 1995.
