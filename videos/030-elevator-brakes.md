## Script

You step into an elevator. Forty floors up. Then you wonder: what if the cable snaps?

First: there is not one cable. There are usually two to six. And each one, alone, can hold the full load, plus 25 percent.

And if the car ever drops faster than it should, a device called a governor trips.

Brake shoes clamp onto the rails, and stop the car. Quickly, but not hard enough to hurt you.

The idea goes back to Elisha Otis. In 1854, he showed off his safety elevator at New York's Crystal Palace, in a dramatic public demo.

Three years later, the first passenger safety elevator opened, in a store in New York City.
