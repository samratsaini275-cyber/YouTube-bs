# Why bridges are built with gaps

**File:** `024-expansion-joints.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 49.9 s

## Description

The gaps in a bridge deck are expansion joints: assemblies that hold the parts together while safely absorbing the expansion and contraction caused by temperature. Finger joints let traffic cross while the deck still moves.

## Script

Ever felt that thump when you drive onto a bridge? That is a gap in the road. And it is there on purpose.

It is called an expansion joint. Because a bridge is not a fixed length.

When it warms up, the materials expand. When it cools down, they shrink back.

With no gap, that movement has nowhere to go. Before joints were used, structures cracked under the stress.

So the deck is built in pieces, joined by steel fingers that slide past each other. Cars cross. The bridge still moves.

Small joints take about 30 millimeters of movement. The biggest are built for more than 3,000.
