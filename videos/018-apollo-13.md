# The switch that nearly killed the Apollo 13 crew

**File:** `018-apollo-13.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 64.1 s

## Description

On April 14, 1970 (UTC), about 330,000 km from Earth, oxygen tank 2 on Apollo 13 failed. Its thermostatic switches were designed for 28 volts but ground equipment supplied 65; overheating during a ground procedure most likely damaged wire insulation. The crew returned safely on April 17.

## Script

Two days into the flight to the Moon, an oxygen tank on Apollo 13 burst. The cause was set up before launch, on the ground.

Inside the tank were heater switches designed for 28 volts. The ground equipment at the launch site supplied 65.

During a ground test, the tank would not empty. So its heaters were switched on, to boil the oxygen off.

Inside, the temperature may have reached 1,000 degrees Fahrenheit. The gauge could not read above 85.

That heat most likely damaged the insulation on wires inside the tank.

In space, 330,000 kilometers from Earth, the crew switched on the tank's fan. The wires arced, and the tank burst.

The three astronauts used the lunar module as a lifeboat.

They splashed down safely on April 17th, 1970.
