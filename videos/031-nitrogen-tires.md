## Script

Jet tires can be pumped to more than 200 pounds per square inch. And filling them with normal air was judged too dangerous.

The worry is the wheels with brakes. Every landing, those brakes work hard.

Inside, the tire's rubber liner can give off gases. Mix those with the oxygen in air, and you risk a chemical reaction. A tire explosion.

So the FAA ordered a fix. On jets like the 737, the 747 and the DC-10, tires on braked wheels get dry nitrogen. Or another inert gas.

The rule? No more than 5 percent oxygen inside.

Stuck at a remote airport with no nitrogen? Air is allowed. But within 15 hours of flying, it has to be swapped out.
