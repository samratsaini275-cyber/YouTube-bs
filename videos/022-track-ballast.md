# Why railway tracks sit on loose stones

**File:** `022-track-ballast.mp4` · 1080x1920 · 49.5 s

## Description

The crushed stone under a railway is called ballast. It bears the load of the ties, rails and trains, restrains sideways movement, drains water and keeps down vegetation. The stones are sharp-edged so that they interlock.

## Script

Why do railway tracks sit on a pile of loose stones? It looks like a shortcut. It is not.

The stones are called ballast. They are packed below, between and around the ties, to carry the weight of the train.

They are not smooth pebbles. They are crushed, with sharp edges. So they lock together, and grip the ties.

A raised shoulder of stone on each side stops the track from sliding sideways.

Rain? It drains straight through the gaps. And the stones keep weeds down.

The layer is never less than 150 millimeters thick. On high speed lines, it can be half a meter.
